PID TRAINER

Vapor at the inlet

Pick a vapor and the monitor responds to it the way a 10.6 eV lamp would. The monitor's own settings do not change.

One-finger shortcuts

You can also hold the real keys: MODE alone for power, MODE and N/− together for Programming Mode.

What the inlet is sampling

Key guide
Power on
Hold MODE until the display starts.
Power off
From the reading screen hold MODE for 3 seconds, then keep holding through the 5-second countdown.
Icons
Battery at the top right. On the line under the reading: the pump (it turns while the pump runs and flashes a warning if stalled) and the datalog disk while logging. With Pump Duty Cycle below 100% and a reading under 2 ppm, the pump icon alternates with a pause mark. With Radio Power on (Monitor Setup, Radio Power), an antenna and five signal bars appear at the top right beside the battery.
Flashlight key
Press the round key to switch the flashlight on. Press again to switch it off.
Timeout
In Programming Mode, 60 seconds with no key press returns to the reading screen without saving the change in progress.
Search mode
Set under Monitor Setup, Op Mode. Sampling starts and stops on request, the second screen shows Avg and Peak in place of TWA and STEL, and an alarm beeps 1 to 7 times a second, faster at higher concentration.
User mode
Basic shows the reading, TWA/STEL/Peak, date and time, gas summary and PC Comm screens. Advanced adds "Change reference?" and "Change M. Gas?" and skips the password.
Alarms
HIGH, OVR and pump stall: 3 beeps a second. LOW: 2. STEL, TWA, CAL (calibration needed) and NEG (reading below the stored zero): 1. Clear only resets the Peak value.
N/−
Steps to the next screen or menu item, moves to the next digit, answers No.
Reverse
Hold N/− for 5 seconds on a screen with a scroll arrow. The arrow flips and N/− then steps backward. Hold 5 seconds again to flip it back.
Y/+
Selects, raises a digit, answers Yes. On the reading screen it tests the alarm, clears a latched alarm, or restarts a stalled pump.
MODE
Backs out, finishes an edit, exits Programming Mode.
Programming
From the reading screen hold MODE and N/− for 3 seconds. Password is 0000, so press MODE (Enter) to confirm. A wrong password opens Calibration only. Advanced user mode skips the password.
Range
Reads 0 to 999.9 ppm in 0.1 ppm steps and 1,000 to 15,000 ppm in 1 ppm steps. Above 15,000 ppm the reading holds at the top of the range and an OVR alarm sounds. The vapor sample slider goes to 16,000 ppm so you can trigger it.
Gas library
222 pre-programmed compounds in the monitor's alphabetical order. Gases with no 10.6 eV correction factor are listed but cannot be selected.
Vapor
Pick a vapor under "Vapor at the inlet" and the slider becomes that vapor's true ppm. The monitor shows what its lamp would report: the true ppm divided by the vapor's correction factor while set to Isobutylene, and the true ppm itself once Meas. Gas is set to the same vapor. Span gas is always 100 ppm isobutylene.
Time multiplier
Speeds up the exposure clock so TWA, STEL, Avg and the datalog count build faster. STEL shows dashes until 15 minutes of exposure time have passed. Readings, countdowns and key holds stay in real time.
Calibrate
Pick the gas under "What the inlet is sampling" first, then run Zero Calib or Span Calib. Bump checks the response against the span gas without changing the calibration. After you change a setting the soft keys switch to Save and Undo: Y/+ saves, N/− discards. Span Calib first asks "Change?": Yes edits the span value, No moves on to applying gas. Countdowns run three times faster than the real 30 seconds.

Screens and menus follow the published user guide and a recorded walkthrough of the monitor. Small details can differ with firmware version. The monitor's gas library and the look-up drop-down both use RAE Systems Technical Note TN-186 (MiniRAE 3000 pre-programmed compounds), 10.6 eV column.

MULTI-GAS TRAINER

One-finger shortcuts

Gas at the inlet

Key guide
Power on
Hold MODE until the display starts. Sensors show "- -" for a few seconds while they warm up (1 to 2 minutes on the real monitor).
Power off
From the reading screen hold MODE and keep holding through the 5-second countdown.
N/−
Hygiene mode steps through Peak, Min, STEL, TWA, date and time, temperature and humidity, battery, runtime, VOC gas status, LEL gas status, and "Communicate With Computer?". Search mode shows Average in place of STEL and TWA.
Y/+
On the reading screen: tests the alarms, clears a latched alarm, or restarts a stalled pump. On Peak or Min it asks "Clear Peak And Minimum Values?".
Programming
Hold MODE and N/− for 3 seconds. Basic user mode asks for the password (0000, press MODE) the first time after power-on. Advanced skips it. Menus: Calibration, Measurement, Alarms, Datalog, Wireless, Monitor.
Response
Sensors respond at the speeds on your quick reference guide: 15 seconds for oxygen and VOC, 25 for CO, 30 for H2S and LEL, and 200 for HCN. Give a reading time to settle and to clear.
Test gases
The HCN cylinder is balanced in nitrogen, so the oxygen reading falls and alarms while it flows. The 4-gas mix holds 18% oxygen, which is below the 19.5% low alarm.
Vapor
In a custom atmosphere, pick the VOC vapor or combustible gas and set its true concentration with the slider or by typing. The monitor shows what its sensor would report: the true amount divided by that gas's correction factor until Meas. Gas is set to the same gas, then 1:1.
Time multiplier
Speeds up the exposure clock so TWA, STEL, Average and the datalog count build faster. Readings, countdowns and key holds stay in real time.
Cross-sensitivity
Sensors also respond to gases other than their own. HCN reads about 6 ppm for every 1 ppm of H2S and 5% of the CO level. CO reads about 20% of the H2S level, and H2S about 1% of the CO level. The VOC sensor sees H2S at one third of its concentration, and it responds to flammable vapors that a 10.6 eV lamp can ionize: the combustible gas you pick also raises the VOC reading by its ppm divided by its PID correction factor. Methane, propane and hydrogen give no PID response. LEL picks up a VOC vapor when flammability data exists for it, plus CO and HCN at small values, and reads low when oxygen is under 10%. With the 4-gas mix flowing, expect HCN to go over range and VOC to show about 3 ppm. Untick the box under "Gas at the inlet" to switch this off.
Calibrate
Pick the gas under "Gas at the inlet" first. Fresh Air needs clean air. Multi Sensor Span uses the 4-gas mix for oxygen, LEL, CO and H2S. HCN and VOC are spanned one at a time from Single Sensor Span with their own cylinders. Each ends with Pass or Fail per sensor. The span math removes the share of each reading that comes from the other gases in a mixed cylinder, so the 4-gas mix gives an exact calibration. Countdowns and sensor response run five times faster during a calibration.
Bump test
The manual method: apply gas on the reading screen and confirm every tested sensor reaches at least its low alarm. The menu and the Y/+ with N/− 5-second shortcut are from later firmware; a sensor that fails shows "Bump" until it passes.
Alarms
High, Over and pump: 3 beeps a second. Low: 2. STEL, TWA, Neg and Cal: 1. Limits: CO 35/200 ppm, oxygen 19.5/23.5%, HCN 4.7/25 ppm, H2S 10/20 ppm, LEL 10/20%, VOC 50/100 ppm. In Search mode the beep rate rises from 1 to 7 a second with concentration.
LEL gases
Change Meas. Gas for LEL lists 97 gases with the MultiRAE correction factors from Technical Note TN-156. The monitor applies the measurement gas factor divided by the calibration gas factor.
Man Down
Turn it on under Alarms, then tick "lying motionless". After the motionless time the monitor asks "Are You OK?". Y/+ cancels, N/− or no answer raises the alarm.

Screens, menus and the alarm table follow the MultiRAE User's Guide (Rev C). Low and high alarm limits, resolution, range and response time are from your agency's quick reference guide (MultiRAE Lite section); calibration gases from TN-114; LEL factors from TN-156; VOC factors from TN-106B. STEL and TWA limits and most default settings are not in those documents and are typical values to confirm against your monitors. Cross-sensitivities are from TN-114 (CO, H2S, HCN), TN-106B (PID) and TN-156 (LEL); where TN-114 gives a range the middle is used.
